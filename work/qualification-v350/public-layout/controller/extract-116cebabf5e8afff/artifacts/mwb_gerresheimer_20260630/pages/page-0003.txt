Net debt and covenants: Balance sheet flexibility remains limited. The financing
side remains a central risk. Net financial debt rose to EUR 2.0bn as of November 30,
2025, from EUR 1,100m in the prior year, mainly reflecting the financing of the
Bormioli Pharma acquisition. Adjusted EBITDA leverage increased to 4.95x from
2.61x, well above the company’s previous medium-term comfort zone. Due to the
delayed publication of the annual report and the resulting technical breach of
contractual requirements, GXI reached supplemental agreements with its lenders in
April 2026. GXI currently does not have to comply with financial covenants.
However, from November 30, 2026 onward, a hard leverage ceiling of 4.75x will
apply. In addition, a new liquidity covenant requires minimum liquidity of EUR 100m.
A breach of these thresholds could trigger early repayment obligations, leaving GXI
with limited room for execution errors.

Centor sale: Disposal of the crown jewel becomes a balance sheet necessity. The
planned sale of Centor Inc. should be viewed less as a discretionary portfolio
optimization and more as a necessary deleveraging measure. Management expects
the transaction to close by the end of FY26, with proceeds intended to stabilize the
capital and financing structure. The risk is that timing, valuation and execution of
the disposal become critical for covenant compliance in an environment of elevated
transparency and refinancing concerns. The company’s liquidity planning for 2026
to 2028 explicitly depends on the successful completion of the Centor sale,
improving revenue and margins, and delivery of the transformation program.

Another new and lowered guidance. The new guidance offers limited relief. GXI
expects FY26 revenue in the lower half of the EUR 2.3bn to EUR 2.4bn range, an
adjusted EBITDA margin of around 17% to 18%, and free cash flow before M&A of
EUR -50m to EUR -100m. Importantly, the guidance at the lower end already reflects
the expectations we had outlined in our last update. In our view, this reduces the
likelihood of a positive reset and confirms that FY26 is likely to remain a year with
limited visibility. The focus on cost reduction, cash management and portfolio
actions is appropriate, but investors will likely require tangible evidence of margin
stabilization and improved free cash flow before reassessing the investment case.

Conclusion. The publication of the FY25 accounts is an important milestone, but not
a clean reset. The reported P/L was materially weaker than expected, the BaFin
review has been expanded, leverage remains high and the new covenant structure
creates a hard boundary from late FY26 onward. The Centor sale could provide
balance sheet relief, but it also introduces a major execution risk around what can
reasonably be called GXI’s crown jewel.  Until there  is tangible progress on
governance, cash flow and deleveraging, the stock should continue to trade with an
elevated risk discount. We incorporate the final figures, update and fine-tune our
model, resulting in a confirmed price target of EUR 12.50. Remains a SELL.
