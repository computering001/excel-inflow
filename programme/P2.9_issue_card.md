## Difference classification
- Intended correction: the label channel reads a caption as alias-plus-qualifier and awards the declared-alias weight to AT MOST ONE role per caption; the percentage inference matches whole words instead of substrings; the resolution is published as `caption_alias_resolution` so the audit says which alias was read and which words were treated as scope
- Regression: none. A repo-wide sweep of 309 captions (every label in every fixture, plus every taxonomy alias in both sections) changed only 4 dispositions: the three registered defects (now accepted as `revenue`), plus `Cash generated from operations` moving unmapped -> ambiguous where its numeric type is inferred. ZERO captions changed accepted role, and ZERO lost acceptance.
- Unresolved: REJECTED APPROACH, recorded because it is the trap this package nearly shipped. Gating the token channel on a role's "mandatory vocabulary" (the words shared by all of its own aliases, e.g. `cost_of_sales` must say "cost") is the register's suggested approach (a) and it does remove the sloppy shared-token evidence. It was implemented, proven to fix all three defects, and REVERTED: this classifier abstains by comparing the leader against the RUNNER-UP, so withholding a competitor's evidence widens the separation margin and makes acceptance EASIER. Proven case from the existing adversarial suite — "Issuer-specific Core operating result" under parent "Core EBITDA": the gate dropped `adjusted_ebitda` 0.76 -> 0.62, and `adjusted_ebit` went from correctly abstaining at margin 0.14 to ACCEPTING at 0.90. EBIT is not EBITDA. Sloppy competitor evidence is load-bearing as an ambiguity signal; in this architecture an evidence-WITHHOLDING repair weakens the margin validator. The reasoning is pinned as a comment at the token channel so the next agent does not re-attempt it. Any future work on the token cap or on token weighting must carry a proof against this case.
