## Which edge, and why it is not the one P4.9 predicted

P4.9 named `statement.tax_expense -> cash.cfo` and predicted a 16-node component. The work order repeated it. **Measurement moved the edge, and the measurement is in the suite.**

- Perturb ONLY the effective tax rate on `loss_making_no_nol_stock` (a plain `forecast_assumptions.effective_tax_rate`, so the perturbation touches one exogenous input). Operating cash flow moves by `-1.7204`. NET INCOME moves by `-1.7204`. The tax charge moves by `+1.7118`. CFO tracks net income EXACTLY and the tax charge NOT AT ALL — the difference is the second-order feedback that also moved pre-tax income.
- The solver says the same thing in source: `fallbackCashFromOperations = netIncome + totalDa + …` and `cashFlowStart = cashFlowGraph.resolveRole("cash_flow_net_income") ?? netIncome`. Neither reads the tax charge.
- And `tax_expense -> cash.cfo` is not even always true: when an issuer DECLARES net income (`operatingGraph.resolveRole("net_income")`), `standaloneNetIncome` never consumes `standaloneTaxCharge`, so cash flow does not depend on the standalone charge at all. Net income is the invariant dependency; the tax charge is a conditional one.

So the landed edges are `edge.net_income_to_cash_flow_start` (`statement_dependency`, `always`) and `edge.net_income_to_cfo` (`cash_flow_bridge`, `always`), and the component is **17 nodes, not 16** — `statement.net_income` measurably moves in 27 of the 33 corpus cases and had to be inside the fixed point too. Landing 16 would have satisfied the work order and violated the invariant.

`edge.net_income_to_cash_flow_start` is, to the letter, the edge P3.3 drove as the mutation that "MUST be caught". P3.3 was right that it closes the loop and wrong that it was hypothetical: the solver has walked it since before P3.3 was written.
